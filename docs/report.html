Why this matters for the data model
  • One product is sold as 6+ sellable offers (1/3/6 units x one-time / subscription), each with its own store code. Revenue per product needs a mapping from every code to one product and a units-per-item factor.
  • A crossed-out list price sits 26% above the 1-unit price: "discount" must be defined once (vs list or vs 1-unit price).
  • Subscriptions start at a discount and renew automatically: active subscriptions and churn need precise definitions (page 6).

On 30 Sep 2026: 11,565 naive vs 11,035 documented (449 paused, 81 past due). Average monthly churn 10.5%.

MetricDefinition (METRICS.md, with SQL)
active_subscriptionstate = active on that day: not paused, no failed payment being retried, not cancelled
active_customer≥ 1 order with positive net revenue in the last 365 days, or an active subscription
churned_subscriptiona cancelled event in the period; voluntary and involuntary split; reactivation counted apart
churn_ratecancellations in month ÷ base at month start (active + paused + past due)
net_revenueorder total after discount − refunds − chargebacks known today, on the order month

Edge cases decided, not left to each chart
  • Paused: not active, stays in the churn base.
  • Payment failed: "past due" while retries run; active again if a retry succeeds, otherwise involuntary churn (11% of all cancellations).
  • Reactivated: active again from that day; its earlier cancellation still counts as churn.

Media Buying's ROAS of 2.45 and Finance's 1.71 are both right: attribution windows explain most of the gap

An investigation from the ad platform down to the chart, with every difference quantified. The agreed numbers for May 2026: first-party ROAS 1.81 gross, 1.71 net, MER 2.87.

SIMULATED
Revenue bridge for paid campaigns, May 2026 (mart_roas_bridge)
The question

Media Buying: "ROAS is 2.45."
Finance: "ROAS is 1.71."

Both are right about different numbers. Walking the number down each layer:

  • 26% of platform value is not backed by a real order (attribution windows).
  • The time zone moves little over a month but 4% of Google-attributed orders change day between LA and NY: it matters for daily pacing.
  • Refunds and chargebacks take another 5.4%.
  • Before the fixes, spend was $3K short of the invoice (new account) and would have been $7K too high from the duplicate load.

Methods, limits and how to reproduce every number

Everything in this report is regenerated from code in the repository.

Data
  • PUBLIC: 7 storefronts (sitemap → product pages, 1 snapshot, 2.5 s between requests, robots.txt respected), Wayback Machine CDX, Google Trends, company press release and careers page. Sources and dates: data/SOURCES.md.
  • SIMULATED: 21 months of orders, subscriptions, payments, shipments, ad delivery, invoices and CRM contacts, built on the real catalogue (src/simulate.py, seed 42). Assumptions: data/ASSUMPTIONS.md.
  • Skipped: Trustpilot (robots.txt disallows crawlers), ad libraries (login), company registry (no clear match).
Methods
  • dbt: staging per source, intermediate business logic, marts per team; seeds for public catalogue and versioned mapping rules; 5 adapter-dispatch macros for DuckDB/BigQuery.
  • Tests: generic (unique, not null, relationships, accepted values) + 12 business rules with stored failures; source freshness.
  • Detection: warehouse replayed without fixes (--vars '{fix_version: 0}'), failing rows matched to a hidden answer key.
  • Report: Python + matplotlib → HTML → PDF (headless Edge).
Limits
  • Simulated data shows methods, not Impact Brands' performance, volumes or systems.
  • VitalPeak shows no price online: assumed price ladder in the simulation.
  • Archive first-capture dates approximate launches; Trends is a relative index.
  • Categories come from product names: reporting labels, not health claims.
  • Dataform models are written for comparison, not run here.
